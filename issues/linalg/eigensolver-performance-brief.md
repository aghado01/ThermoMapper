## Solver selection

Choose one production solver from the representation, the requested spectrum and the accuracy the caller needs. A dense symmetric matrix that needs all eigenpairs fits the optimized full-spectrum solver. A matrix-free operator with a request for a few extremal pairs fits LOBPCG. E8 also has a gradient-factor input whose weak directions are read by thin SVD. Timings can resolve cost crossovers among suitable methods. The JIT specializes the selected implementation for the host. ## 1. Make the two failure cases reproducible and fix them

- **LOBPCG residual at the iteration cap.** The loop measures the residual on the current block, then can return the updated block with that earlier measurement. Reproduce with the symmetric tridiagonal matrix with diagonal `[4, 3, 2]` and adjacent entries 1, largest pair, seed 173, tolerance `1e-12` and one iteration. In the review, the result reported 0.76200174 while its returned pair gave 0.34933228. Make `ResidualNorm` and `Converged` describe the returned eigenpairs. Verify by independently applying the operator to those pairs on capped and converged runs. [Iteration and return](../../src/maths/linalg/Lobpcg.cs)
- **EigenFast sweep with no rotations.** Use a 32 × 32 matrix with diagonal 1 through 32 and every off-diagonal `5e-13`, at the existing `1e-12` tolerance. Every pivot is skipped while their aggregate norm exceeds the sweep exit threshold. Caps of 1 and 256 produced identical eigenpairs in the review. Make a sweep that performs no rotations terminate. Gate this with a deterministic check of work performed, plus residual and orthogonality checks on the witness and on inputs that require rotations. Keep the existing pivot tolerance for this fix. ### E8 leading output patterns

`OutputPosterior.Of` already avoids forming the dense output covariance. It computes a thin SVD of each draw's factor, then another on the combined weighted factors. Its `patterns` argument limits the returned pattern vectors. `MeanEigenvalues` exposes the full nonzero spectrum of the posterior mean, and `MeanTrace` sums it. Benchmark a separately requested leading-patterns readout for large output dimension and small requested rank. Apply the mean operator as `v ↦ Σₛ wₛ Fₛ(Fₛᵀv)` and accumulate its trace as `Σₛ wₛ ‖Fₛ‖²_F`. [Output posterior](../../src/maths/regression/subspaces/OutputSide.cs)

Use two equally weighted draws with covariances `diag(4,3,0)` and `diag(0,3,4)`. Their mean is `diag(2,3,2)`, so the second coordinate leads even though it ranks second in each draw. Compare the operator result with the current SVD on this case and on clustered leading eigenvalues. Check the trace, Ritz residuals and leading subspace angles. Record end-to-end time, allocations and operator applications as the factor width, output dimension and requested rank vary. For a high-dimensional input caller requesting only the first few directions, the analogous experiment applies `Gᵀ(Gv)/M` for `M` equally weighted gradient rows. E8's thin SVD still serves requests that resolve weak directions from that factor. Exact g-prior evidence and its spectral sums require the full pencil spectrum. ### Relative tolerance and the regression transforms

Consider a LinAlg entry point for a symmetric dense solve with a relative tolerance. E8's `ScaledEigen` divides the matrix by its largest absolute entry, solves at `1e-14`, then restores the eigenvalue scale. `PriorPencil` first whitens against the prior and scales the whitened matrix by its largest diagonal for a `1e-15` solve. `RidgeSpectrum` standardizes each column separately to form a unit-diagonal Gram. That standardization defines the normalized ridge prior. Keep the statistical transforms, input checks and tolerances with their callers. Qualify shared solver arithmetic by rescaling a fixed matrix and checking residuals in the original units, then compare pencil evidence and ridge shrinkage with the existing paths.
