What Kass already ships
DiMatteo, Genovese, and Kass (Biometrika, 2001) is the method: free-knot splines, reversible-jump MCMC on the knot set, Laplace integration of the coefficients, local proposals, unit-information prior on the coefficients given the knots. Wallstrom, Liebner, and Kass (JSS, 2008) is the implementation: C for the normal and Poisson cases, LAPACK/BLAS, S and R wrappers, still hosted at stat.cmu.edu/~kass/bars, plus a Matlab Poisson port. There is no maintained PyPI package of that method. lanl/pyBASS (pybass-emu) is Francom–Sansó Bayesian adaptive spline surfaces, a different model. The unofficial R package aashen12/BARS is not a substitute. ⁠Stat.cmu +2

Your module is not a re-skin of that C core. docs/Maths/BayesianRegression.md and src/maths/regression/ are a managed reimplementation: BarsConfig / Bars.Run → BarsEnsemble, clamped B-splines and step basis, banded $Z^\top W Z$ with LDLᵀ, complexity priors, local-beta and adaptive knot kernels, DMGK tempering, and observation models beyond Kass (weighted normal with exact unit-information marginal, Poisson, Whittle, Gibbs loss, Student-t and contaminated-normal weights). The piece Kass does not have is SplineExtrema: argmax, significant peaks, significant peak spans, PeakPosterior, PeakMode, PeakSpan. BAPS (including the hierarchical P-spline), BGP, and exact changepoint sit in the same module. Maths.Regression does not reference SPC. The caller supplies the curve. That is the package boundary.

What the lean notes actually license you to claim
lean/Protolemmata/BARS/ is a protolemma ledger, not compiled proofs, and it deliberately does not formalize the sampler. spc-bars-readout-obligations.md (2026-08-08) splits the seam the packages should copy: BARS supplies posterior spline draws and candidate/crossing arithmetic; SPC supplies the meaning of a thermal peak, the boundary policy, the prominence threshold, the span level, and clipped spans. Target module name there is Spc.BarsReadout.⁠GitHub
Three pillars in fable-BARS-lemma.md, only one of which is the publication claim:

Approximation economy (sparse-grid SW plus spline versus dense-grid SW) needs a Jackson-type bound. Not fishable in Mathlib today. Do not claim it.
Posterior contraction / $\hat R$ stopping is an MCMC-convergence statement. A research program, not a lemma. Do not advertise Lean-verified MCMC.
Exact analytic ROI is the fishable original contribution. A maximizer of a non-constant polynomial on a closed span is an endpoint or a root of the derivative. For a cubic the derivative is degree at most 2, so the candidate set is finite and closed-form. The grid scan is vestigial. SplineExtrema is the implementation of this.⁠GitHub

Two facts from the span and multipeak notes have to travel into the Python API, because they are why the demo is not “smooth $\chi(T)$ and argmax”:

Argmax and expectation do not commute (the two-tent counterexample). Peaks are taken per draw, then pooled. PeakPosterior is the object; the mode of the mean curve is not. Span endpoints are nearest level crossings of $f=\ell$ on monotone brackets. FWHM of the mean curve is not the mean of the FWHMs, so spans are never read off r.Fit.

O1–O3 are still open as formal deliverables: boundary-aware argmax, finite candidate union $\{\text{endpoints}\}\cup\{\text{knots}\}\cup\{\text{interior derivative roots}\}$ plus neighbour-test faithfulness, and a prominence-gated readout equivalent to SplineExtrema (interior-only vs include-boundary, prominence $\ge$ relativeProminence $\cdot(\max-\min)$, crossing level $h-\rho(h-c)$, clipping as a missing in-domain crossing rather than a fabricated root). The Python pair can ship that contract as tested code while the Lean stays a spec.

Fix before a release
docs/Maths/BayesianRegression.md records a stationary-distribution mismatch: the target is $\log p(y\mid Z,W)+\log p(k)$ with no $\xi\mid k$ density, but birth uses Lebesgue measure on the ordered simplex (volume $1/k!$), so the chain’s stationary is proportional to $p(k)/k!$, not $p(k)$. Fix the prior or document the $1/k!$ factor before the pip package claims to implement the stated target. Also already stated in that doc: do not combine adaptive $\tau$ with a weight resampler, and grids must be pre-scaled to $[0,1]$.

The module's own opening is four families that share one strategy: exploit Gaussian conjugacy in the coefficients (integrate them out, or draw them from closed-form conditionals), then sample or select the remaining hyperparameters.

BAPS: penalized splines, including the hierarchical P-spline and the anisotropic tensor product.
BGP: a Gaussian process whose bandwidth is sampled under an empirical-Bayes prior.

The Green lineage is real, and it sits in the sampler, not in the spline family. docs/Maths/Samplers.md is where it lives: ReversibleJumpChain implements Green's acceptance ratio split by responsibility into target ratio, move-selection ratio, auxiliary-density ratio, and Jacobian. The engine is model-agnostic. BARS birth, death, and relocate are IRjMove plugs; the DMGK schedule is a decorator on the move weights; tempering is a target decorator so the Hastings terms stay untempered. Other clients of that engine already exist (InverseTransform, SpectralNoiseField), which is another reason not to brand the engine as BARS. The citation is Green, Biometrika 1995, for dimension-jumping MCMC. His running example there was multiple changepoints, which is why the lineage reaches this module twice: once as the engine BARS plugs into, and once as the problem ExactChangepoint solves by dynamic programming rather than by Green's sampler.
