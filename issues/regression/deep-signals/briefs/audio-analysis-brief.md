# Signal processing for inspectable audio regression

Conversation audio is the eventual training data. The immediate question is what the regression explains in a recording and what structure survives in its residual. The pilot already analyses synthetic resonances and harmonic sources, takes windowed log powers, and reports correlation between errors on neighbouring frequency bins. ## First-pass report — 2026-11-16

The pilot's plotted error is **predicted minus clean log power** at each analysis frequency. Its lag-one statistic runs along frequency within each held-out code. LPC instead predicts the next waveform sample and yields a signed error in time. Labeling the two quantities and their units gives the visualizer a concrete first requirement. The checks below are proposed experiments. [Pilot error calculation](../../receipts/latent-recovery/probe/Pilot.cs) · [Waveform and feature definitions](../../receipts/latent-recovery/probe/Synthesizer.cs)

**Measuring window.** A stationary tone traces the shifted frequency response of the window. At fixed sample rate, a longer window narrows its main lobe in Hz and spreads a transient across a longer time interval. A Hann taper suppresses distant sidelobes and widens the main lobe relative to a rectangular window. For a bin-centred complex tone, amplitude gain is Σw. For zero-mean white noise of variance σ², mean power before the log floor is σ²Σw². The pilot's unit-energy periodic Hann therefore holds the noise scale fixed while coherent-tone peak power grows with frame length. Overlapping frames reuse samples. A shorter hop gives denser time readings with correlated errors. [Harris (1978), §§II–III and Table I](https://amst.ece.iastate.edu/paper/comparative_study/window_1.pdf)

*Display and check.* Hold the waveform and fitted prediction fixed. Show the observed and predicted log powers and their signed difference on matched axes, with frame duration, hop, taper and log floor visible. At 8 kHz, move a steady tone half a Fourier bin off centre and compare 256- and 512-sample rectangular and periodic Hann windows on the same dense Hz grid. Measure main-lobe width in Hz, peak loss relative to each window's on-bin tone, and the mean power of a white-noise control before logging. Halve the hop and compare the values at shared frame centres, then measure adjacent-frame correlation on the noise control. Keep this temporal correlation separate from the pilot's adjacent-frequency statistic. The pilot already measured window-dependent turnover for damped resonances. [Existing window sweep](../../latent-recovery-pilot-brief.md)

**Speech prediction.** Over a short, approximately stationary segment, fit the all-pole predictor and compute e[n] = x[n] − Σ aⱼx[n−j]. A voiced vowel can leave large errors near successive glottal closures and a peak in residual autocorrelation near the pitch period. Turbulent excitation in an unvoiced fricative can leave a more diffuse residual without a stable pitch lag. Mixed excitation, source shape and vocal-tract mismatch can leave structure in either case. Atal and Hanauer distinguish their pulse/noise synthesis model from the measured prediction error in their analysis. [Atal and Hanauer (1971), §II and Figs. 2, 5](https://people.engr.tamu.edu/rgutier/web_courses/cpsc689_s07/atalHanauer1971lpc.pdf)

*Display and check.* Align the waveform, one-step prediction and signed residual. On a synthetic resonator driven in turn by pulses 8 ms apart and by white noise, fit the same short-time predictor order and compare residual autocorrelation at 8 ms and prediction gain in dB on a held-out segment. Then repeat on a sustained vowel and a fricative. The pilot's harmonic synthesizer supplies known pitch, but its sum of sinusoids has no annotated glottal closures. A recording with a synchronized electroglottograph (EGG) can independently check closure timing. Brookes et al. show that opening events can produce additional candidates even during voiced speech. [Brookes, Naylor and Gudnason (2006), §§IV–V](https://spiral.imperial.ac.uk/server/api/core/bitstreams/30c42f57-d949-4ffd-8011-3a5dc4c47b42/content)

**Sparse innovations.** In the noiseless periodic model, K distinct Diracs with nonzero weights in a known period have Fourier coefficients that are sums of K complex exponentials. A suitable ideal low-pass (sinc) measurement supplies consecutive Fourier coefficients. An annihilating filter recovers the locations modulo the period, then a linear system recovers the weights. The paper also gives a Gaussian-kernel construction for finite Dirac streams and separate constructions for splines and piecewise polynomials. The kernel and linear sample values are part of each result. The pilot's floored log powers discard the phase needed for this location argument. [Vetterli, Marziliano and Blu (2002), §§III–IV, Theorems 1, 2, 4 and 6](https://bigwww.epfl.ch/publications/vetterli0201.pdf)

*Display and check.* Start with two known sub-sample events in one period. Generate their noiseless complex low-pass coefficients, fit locations and signed weights, then repeat while reducing event separation and adding noise. Overlay the recovered times and weights on the truth and report matched timing error, weight error, and errors on withheld complex coefficients. For speech, try the model first on voiced LPC residuals. Evidence to proceed would be reproducible timing against EGG after delay alignment, reported misses and extra detections, and lower held-out waveform error than a regular pulse train at the same event budget. Fricatives provide a control for spurious periodic detections. [FRI measurement model](https://bigwww.epfl.ch/publications/vetterli0201.pdf) · [EGG timing and detection criteria](https://spiral.imperial.ac.uk/server/api/core/bitstreams/30c42f57-d949-4ffd-8011-3a5dc4c47b42/content)

**Open questions.** Which portion of adjacent-bin error persists when both waveforms are remeasured with another taper? Across speakers, which LPC orders and frame durations retain pitch timing without fitting away the excitation? Is a fixed pulse shape or sampling kernel adequate across a held-out voiced segment?
