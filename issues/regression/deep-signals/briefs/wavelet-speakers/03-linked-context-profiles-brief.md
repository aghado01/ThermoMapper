# 3. Learn two recurring acoustic profiles from unlabeled pairings

Proposed population-identification slice. Use [the measured harmonic fraction](01-harmonic-measurements-brief.md) as a scalar response \(T\) in a finite set of observed acoustic contexts. A speaker's profile assigns each context a predicted response. This permits two speakers to agree closely in one context and separate in another.

**Training information.** Each record contains two segments known by construction to share a source, with a prespecified pair of contexts \((u,v)\). Across records, the unobserved source \(S\) takes values \(+1,-1\) with equal probability, independently of the selected edge. Given source and contexts, the two responses are independent, with means \(\theta_{s,u}=\mu_u+s a_u\) in \((0,1)\). The learner receives context tags, observed responses and their within-record pairing. The context tags can be controlled synthesis settings. Acquiring equivalent tags or reliable pairings from speech is a separate inference problem. Assume every context is sampled.

**Identification claim.** Marginal means give \(\mu_u\). A paired observation gives the centered cross moment \(a_u a_v\). On a connected graph of distinct-context pairs with all \(a_u\ne0\), prove that an odd cycle determines both profiles up to a single exchange of speakers. Derive the absolute contrast at a cycle vertex by alternating products and quotients of the measured moments, then propagate the remaining contrasts. A same-context pair of *independently drawn segments* supplies \(a_u^2\) and can replace the odd cycle. Squaring a single observed segment instead would include its conditional variance. Handle zero-contrast contexts by stating which informative components have relative label alignment.

**Necessity on actual observed laws.** On a connected bipartite graph with no diagonal pairs, scaling \(a\) by \(c>0\) on one side and by \(1/c\) on the other preserves all edge products. Realize an interior example with unequal profiles beyond global exchange and identical full pair laws. Use two attainable harmonic fractions \(0<l<h<1\) and a random draw \(T\in\{l,h\}\) with success probability \((\theta_{s,u}-l)/(h-l)\). The means and product moment determine each two-by-two table. Synthesize signals at the two fractions with a fixed pitch and phase, and check equality for their wavelet observations. Contrast a path of three contexts with the triangle obtained by adding the missing pair. Show one subsequent acoustic prediction, for example the law of a paired observation on the missing edge, on which the path-compatible models disagree.

**Confounding control.** Construct an experiment in which all available pairs share both speaker and a binary recording condition. A response generated by the condition alone can match the speaker-generated training observations. Show how crossing the recording condition while keeping the speaker changes an observable in that example. State the needed independence at the pairing step.

**Closure.** The graph ingredient is established rank-one completion theory. The deliverable is the criterion instantiated on realized acoustic pair laws and a prediction changed by the missing pairing. Exact population recovery here assumes a two-speaker balanced mixture. Reuse [phase propagation](../../../../../lean/Lemmas/TimeFrequency/PhasePropagation.lean) where appropriate. ## Slice 3 result and proof report

On a connected graph of observed context pairs with nonzero contrasts, one odd cycle or one independently drawn same-context pair identifies both source mean profiles up to exchanging their names. This is an exact population statement. It assumes known context tags, reliable same-source pairing, a balanced source draw independent of the chosen contexts, and conditional independence of the two responses given source and contexts. A finite sample or an unlabeled recording without this pairing information requires further assumptions.

### What can be read from the pair laws

For a context pair \((u,v)\), the observed law is the equal mixture of the two source-specific product measures. Its marginal means are \(\mu_u,\mu_v\). Its centered cross moment is \(m_{uv}=a_u a_v\). A fresh same-context pair yields \(a_u^2\). Squaring one observation and subtracting \(\mu_u^2\) instead yields \(a_u^2\) **plus the average of the two within-source variances**. The moment and identification proofs allow arbitrary integrable conditional response laws, with finite second moments for the single-observation variance calculation. [Population laws and profile identification](../../../../../lean/Enthymemata/TimeFrequency/ProfilePairMoments.lean)

On an odd closed walk starting at \(r\), number the measured moments \(m_0,\ldots,m_{2k}\) in traversal order. Then
\[
|a_r|=\sqrt{\frac{m_0 m_2\cdots m_{2k}}{m_1 m_3\cdots m_{2k-1}}}.
\]
Choose either sign at the root and propagate by \(a_v=m_{uv}/a_u\). The two choices exchange the source names everywhere. A diagonal repeat supplies the same root magnitude directly. The proof reuses the existing one-vertex phase-propagation theorem to certify that every compatible real solution has one of these two orientations. [Alternating quotients and propagation](../../../../../lean/Enthymemata/TimeFrequency/ProfilePairGraph.lean) · [Phase propagation](../../../../../lean/Lemmas/TimeFrequency/PhasePropagation.lean)

When a contrast vanishes, the two means there coincide. On the graph induced by the nonzero contrasts, labels align along each connected component. Edges incident to zeros have zero product, so separate components can exchange labels independently. Each informative component needs its own magnitude anchor for full recovery. On a bipartite component without a repeat, multiplying contrasts on one side by \(c>0\) and dividing those on the other by \(c\) preserves every edge product. These component statements concern the true nonzero support. A zero product by itself does not identify which endpoint vanishes. The underlying rank-one graph criterion has prior art. [Ma, Olshevsky, Szepesvári and Saligrama (2020)](https://jmlr.org/papers/v21/19-359.html)

### A missing acoustic prediction

At all three contexts, take \(\mu=1/2\). Each segment has harmonic fraction \(1/4\) or \(3/4\), with high-fraction probability \((\theta-1/4)/(3/4-1/4)\) at conditional mean \(\theta\). Both outcomes are synthesized as 24-sample blocks at pitch bin 3 with zero phases. Their normalized three-scale Haar observations are distinct. Conditional independence and the fair source mixture give a four-cell table on each edge. The marginal means and the single product \(a_u a_v\) determine all four cells. [Binary laws, conditional means and their pushforwards](../../../../../lean/Enthymemata/TimeFrequency/ProfileBinaryLaws.lean)

| Model | Contrasts at contexts 0, 1, 2 | Probability of two high readings on either training edge | Probability on the missing edge (0, 2) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A | \(1/16,\ 1/16,\ 1/16\) | \(17/64\) | \(17/64\) |
| B | \(1/8,\ 1/32,\ 1/8\) | \(17/64\) | \(5/16\) |

The path \(0\!-\!1\!-\!2\) gives identical **complete pair laws** for A and B, both for raw synthesized blocks and for their Haar measurements. The profiles differ even after a global exchange. Adding the pair \((0,2)\) changes the predicted probability of two high readings by \(3/64\). On the resulting triangle the proof also covers arbitrary admissible binary-response candidates with different centers. Equality of all three measured pair laws forces equality of the profiles up to one exchange. ### Crossing the recording condition

Draw the speaker and the first recording condition as independent fair bits. Each segment has high-fraction probability \(3/4\) or \(1/4\), selected entirely by one of those bits. If both bits persist across the pair, the speaker-driven and condition-driven models give the same complete training law. Holding the speaker fixed and switching the condition gives two high Haar readings with probability **\(5/16\)** for the speaker-driven model and **\(3/16\)** for the condition-driven model. Independently redrawing the second condition gives probability **\(1/4\)** under the condition-driven model. Thus a matched-source acquisition needs to control or independently randomize persistent recording conditions for the conditional product-law assumption to apply. [Recording-condition experiment](../../../../../lean/Enthymemata/TimeFrequency/ProfilePairConfounding.lean)
