# 3. Learn two recurring acoustic profiles from unlabeled pairings

Proposed population-identification slice. Use [the measured harmonic fraction](01-harmonic-measurements-brief.md) as a scalar response \(T\) in a finite set of observed acoustic contexts. A speaker's profile assigns each context a predicted response. This permits two speakers to agree closely in one context and separate in another.

**Training information.** Each record contains two segments known by construction to share a source, with a prespecified pair of contexts \((u,v)\). Across records, the unobserved source \(S\) takes values \(+1,-1\) with equal probability, independently of the selected edge. Given source and contexts, the two responses are independent, with means \(\theta_{s,u}=\mu_u+s a_u\) in \((0,1)\). The learner receives context tags, observed responses and their within-record pairing. The context tags can be controlled synthesis settings. Acquiring equivalent tags or reliable pairings from speech is a separate inference problem. Assume every context is sampled.

**Identification claim.** Marginal means give \(\mu_u\). A paired observation gives the centered cross moment \(a_u a_v\). On a connected graph of distinct-context pairs with all \(a_u\ne0\), prove that an odd cycle determines both profiles up to a single exchange of speakers. Derive the absolute contrast at a cycle vertex by alternating products and quotients of the measured moments, then propagate the remaining contrasts. A same-context pair of *independently drawn segments* supplies \(a_u^2\) and can replace the odd cycle. Squaring a single observed segment instead would include its conditional variance. Handle zero-contrast contexts by stating which informative components have relative label alignment.

**Necessity on actual observed laws.** On a connected bipartite graph with no diagonal pairs, scaling \(a\) by \(c>0\) on one side and by \(1/c\) on the other preserves all edge products. Realize an interior example with unequal profiles beyond global exchange and identical full pair laws. Use two attainable harmonic fractions \(0<l<h<1\) and a random draw \(T\in\{l,h\}\) with success probability \((\theta_{s,u}-l)/(h-l)\). The means and product moment determine each two-by-two table. Synthesize signals at the two fractions with a fixed pitch and phase, and check equality for their wavelet observations. Contrast a path of three contexts with the triangle obtained by adding the missing pair. Show one subsequent acoustic prediction, for example the law of a paired observation on the missing edge, on which the path-compatible models disagree.

**Confounding control.** Construct an experiment in which all available pairs share both speaker and a binary recording condition. A response generated by the condition alone can match the speaker-generated training observations. Show how crossing the recording condition while keeping the speaker changes an observable in that example. State the needed independence at the pairing step.

**Closure.** The graph ingredient is established rank-one completion theory. The deliverable is the criterion instantiated on realized acoustic pair laws and a prediction changed by the missing pairing. Exact population recovery here assumes a two-speaker balanced mixture. Reuse [phase propagation](../../../../../lean/Lemmas/TimeFrequency/PhasePropagation.lean) where appropriate.
