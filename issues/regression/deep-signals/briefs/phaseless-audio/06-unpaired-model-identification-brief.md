# Slice 6 — model sets and laws from unpaired windows

**Question.** Suppose each training observation carries its operator label, and the record linking different views of one utterance is lost. What can separate image sets or separate probability laws identify? State and prove finite criteria that can actually be applied to the audio windows. [Paired witness](05-paired-model-identification-brief.md) · [Tachella source and operator assumptions](README.md)

Let C be a known finite dictionary of distinct real waveforms modulo sign and Mⱼ a nonempty finite family of views on C. For view j use the exact, finite alphabet Mⱼ(C). One observation regime supplies the image set Yⱼ of each view. The other supplies the marginal probability on each cell of each alphabet. Assume exact population observations.

1. **Image sets.** Given an unknown X ⊆ C and Yⱼ = Mⱼ(X), define H = {c ∈ C | Mⱼ(c) ∈ Yⱼ for every j}. Prove that H is the largest compatible support. Characterize uniqueness among all subsets of C: H = X and every x ∈ X has a view in which its measured value is attained by no other member of X. Show both failure modes by constructing a larger compatible support from a point in H ∖ X, or a smaller one by removing a point without such a view. The second condition matters even when H = X.
2. **Marginal laws.** Form the matrix D with a row per labeled measurement value (j,b), a column per candidate c, and entry 1 when Mⱼ(c)=b. Prove that Dp lists all marginals of a probability vector p. Prove injectivity over the whole probability simplex exactly when ker D has no nonzero vector of total mass zero. A nonzero kernel direction of zero total mass yields equal marginals after normalizing its positive and negative parts. With at least one complete view block, summing that block's rows already gives total mass.
3. **Two controls.** Apply both tests to the four-candidate example from slice 5. Exhibit the missing association and the signed kernel vector for the two laws. Take any three of those four waveforms as a second dictionary. Verify that each view still has a collision and the separate marginal probabilities determine all three masses. On this same dictionary, exhibit a singleton support determined by its image sets and a proper two-element subset with the same separate image sets as the full dictionary. This tests exactly what is lost when only the occupied bins are retained.

**Done when** both criteria have proofs and positive and negative instances using the specified Fourier views. In the report, distinguish identifiability for a fixed finite dictionary from Tachella et al.'s generic linear-operator theorem for low-dimensional signal sets. For an unrestricted audio class, name the missing separation or model assumption and retain any counterexample that lies in that class.

## Verified result — 2026-11-16

For a known finite dictionary of distinct waveform sign classes and a nonempty finite family of fixed labeled views, the following criteria use exact population observations. [UnpairedModels.lean](../../../../../lean/Enthymemata/TimeFrequency/UnpairedModels.lean)

- **Occupied values.** The hull contains every candidate whose value in each view occurs in the corresponding observed set. `hull_images` shows that the hull itself reproduces all image sets, and `compatible_subset_hull` places every compatible support inside it. `support_unique_iff` proves that X is the unique support exactly when its hull equals X and each x in X has a view value attained by x alone within X. `insert_images` constructs a larger compatible support from any extra hull point. `erase_images` removes a member lacking a private value while preserving all image sets. These implications also cover the empty support.
- **Marginal probabilities.** The rows of D are labeled pairs (j,b), with b in the exact finite image of the full dictionary under view j. A column has one entry of one in each view block. `incidence_marginal` identifies Dp with the measured masses. `simplex_injective_iff` proves identification over the entire simplex exactly when the kernel contains no nonzero zero-sum vector. For any such vector, `kernel_witness` normalizes its positive and negative parts by their common positive mass, producing distinct probability vectors with identical marginals. Summing one complete block gives total mass, so every kernel vector already has zero total mass (`block_mass`, `complete_block_kernel`).
- **Four candidates.** Use the order (1,2), (2,−1), (1,−2), (2,1). The singleton view returns A=(1,2) or B=(2,1), and the two-sample view returns P=(3,1) or Q=(1,3). The rows in candidate order are AP, BQ, AQ, BP. `four_physical_views` checks these codes against the Fourier magnitudes, and `decode_injective` certifies the relabeling in each view. The supports {0,1}, {2,3} and the full dictionary occupy the same bins. The direction (1,1,−1,−1) lies in the kernel, and its normalized parts give the two equiprobable models in slice 5 (`four_supports`, `four_kernel`). The full dictionary equals its hull but has no private values.
- **Three candidates.** Retain indices 0,1,2. Both views still have collisions. The P row gives p₀, the B row gives p₁, and the A row gives p₀+p₂. Thus the labeled marginals recover every mass, proved even for arbitrary real weights by `three_injective`. With only occupied bins, the singleton {2} is uniquely determined by A and Q. The proper subset {0,1} occupies all four labeled bins, just as all three candidates do (`three_supports`). [Fourier witness and paired event](05-paired-model-identification-brief.md)

Tachella, Chen and Davies assume a signal set satisfying their dimension hypothesis A1. Their multiple-operator theorem gives model-set uniqueness for almost every family of G linear maps from ℝⁿ to ℝᵐ if m>k+n/G. Here the nonlinear Fourier views and the finite dictionary are fixed, and the incidence test also covers probability laws. For a larger audio class, identification needs separation of compatible supports or injectivity of the separate pushforwards on its admissible laws. Every class containing the four representatives retains the two-model counterexample for these two views. [Multiple-operator theorem](https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.12513v2)
