# Slice 6 — model sets and laws from unpaired windows

**Question.** Suppose each training observation carries its operator label, and the record linking different views of one utterance is lost. What can separate image sets or separate probability laws identify? State and prove finite criteria that can actually be applied to the audio windows. [Paired witness](05-paired-model-identification-brief.md) · [Tachella source and operator assumptions](README.md)

Let C be a known finite dictionary of distinct real waveforms modulo sign and Mⱼ a nonempty finite family of views on C. For view j use the exact, finite alphabet Mⱼ(C). One observation regime supplies the image set Yⱼ of each view. The other supplies the marginal probability on each cell of each alphabet. Assume exact population observations.

1. **Image sets.** Given an unknown X ⊆ C and Yⱼ = Mⱼ(X), define H = {c ∈ C | Mⱼ(c) ∈ Yⱼ for every j}. Prove that H is the largest compatible support. Characterize uniqueness among all subsets of C: H = X and every x ∈ X has a view in which its measured value is attained by no other member of X. Show both failure modes by constructing a larger compatible support from a point in H ∖ X, or a smaller one by removing a point without such a view. The second condition matters even when H = X.
2. **Marginal laws.** Form the matrix D with a row per labeled measurement value (j,b), a column per candidate c, and entry 1 when Mⱼ(c)=b. Prove that Dp lists all marginals of a probability vector p. Prove injectivity over the whole probability simplex exactly when ker D has no nonzero vector of total mass zero. A nonzero kernel direction of zero total mass yields equal marginals after normalizing its positive and negative parts. With at least one complete view block, summing that block's rows already gives total mass.
3. **Two controls.** Apply both tests to the four-candidate example from slice 5. Exhibit the missing association and the signed kernel vector for the two laws. Take any three of those four waveforms as a second dictionary. Verify that each view still has a collision and the separate marginal probabilities determine all three masses. On this same dictionary, exhibit a singleton support determined by its image sets and a proper two-element subset with the same separate image sets as the full dictionary. This tests exactly what is lost when only the occupied bins are retained.

**Done when** both criteria have proofs and positive and negative instances using the specified Fourier views. In the report, distinguish identifiability for a fixed finite dictionary from Tachella et al.'s generic linear-operator theorem for low-dimensional signal sets. For an unrestricted audio class, name the missing separation or model assumption and retain any counterexample that lies in that class.
