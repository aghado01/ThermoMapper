# Slice 4 — conditioning in the recorded powers and log powers

**Question.** Can a weak connection between two pieces of audio explain a large reconstruction error under a small perturbation of the *recorded* cells? Use [slice 2](02-weighted-cut-brief.md) for edge products and [slice 3](03-finite-window-identifiability-brief.md) for the actual finite power map P. The [Grohs–Rathmair source](README.md) gives a continuous theorem for Gaussian Gabor magnitudes. This assignment uses finite Euclidean norms.

1. **Necessity from a sign flip.** With x = u+v and y = u−v on disjoint supports, compute the exact squared measured discrepancy, 16 Σⱼ Re((Aⱼu) conj(Aⱼv))², and the squared sign-quotient distance, 4 min(‖u‖₂², ‖v‖₂²). Every such pair in the chosen class supplies an upper bound on the best lower stability constant. Exhibit an exact or limiting weak cut. A small cut can disprove a proposed uniform guarantee. A lower stability bound requires control over every admissible pair.
2. **When a graph certifies measured stability.** State and prove a transfer lemma. For instance, if a fixed linear map B extracts the chosen edge products from the raw power vector, then ‖Δq‖₂ ≤ ‖B‖ ‖ΔP‖₂. Combine this with slice 2 for fixed amplitudes and instantiate B on the length-two windows. For a proposed Hann graph, produce a valid extraction or domination bound, or exhibit an obstruction in a stated geometry. A retained cell measuring only DC on three unit-weight samples is a simple test: two sign patterns can give the same DC power and different adjacent products. State exactly which graph, weights and operator a certificate covers.
3. **Amplitude variation and the log feature.** On a stated real signal class C use d± from slice 2 and the infimum of ‖P(x)−P(y)‖₂ / d±(x,y) over inequivalent pairs. Show why a scale-free positive bound fails on a class containing 0 and arbitrarily small multiples of a visible waveform. Work out a positive control allowing varying amplitudes, e.g. the singleton power measurement on a real scalar with |x| ≥ a > 0. On a class with every cell power in [0,U], prove the two Lipschitz bounds for log(P + ε) with constants 1/(U+ε) and 1/ε, and carry them through the data norm. If a positive lower bound κ holds in that norm, derive the waveform error bound for an observation with noise at most η and a fitted candidate with residual at most ρ.

**Done when** the cut witness, conditional transfer, scale obstruction, positive control and perturbation bound have statements with explicit norms and hypotheses. Report the strongest claim justified for the sampled pilot, even if it is a failure of stability on the unrestricted waveform space. A clustering statistic becomes a certificate only for a geometry where the comparison has been proved.

## Verified result — 2026-11-16

[MeasuredStability.lean](../../../../../lean/Enthymemata/TimeFrequency/MeasuredStability.lean) uses Euclidean norms for waveform, edge products, retained powers and log features. Waveform error is the minimum distance to either signed representative.

- `cut_data_sq` gives the squared measured discrepancy of u+v and u−v as 16 Σⱼ Re((Aⱼu) conj(Aⱼv))². `cut_distance_sq` gives the squared waveform discrepancy as four times the lesser squared norm of u and v. `lowerConstant_spec` characterizes the infimum over pairs at positive quotient distance when that set is nonempty. `cut_upper_bound` makes each admissible cut an upper bound. On the three-sample path (1,δ,1), flipping the last sign gives squared waveform error 4 and squared raw two-sample power discrepancy 32δ². This family rules out a uniform positive lower bound as δ approaches zero. [Slice 2 witness](02-weighted-cut-brief.md)
- `extraction_bound` gives the Euclidean operator-norm comparison for any fixed linear extraction. `fixedAmplitude_transfer` applies the slice 2 cut constant to competitors with the same absolute sample values. For any finite simple graph with one unnormalized rectangular length-two DC/Nyquist frame per undirected edge, take (p₀−p₁)/4. `pairExtract_exact`, `pairExtract_bound`, `edge_extraction` and `fixedAmplitude_edgePowers` give 8 h d±² ≤ ‖ΔP‖₂², where h uses vertex masses xᵢ² and crossing weights xᵢ²xₖ². The factor eight is attained by differences proportional to (1,−1) in each changing frame.
- `dc_extraction_obstruction` compares (1,1,−1) with (1,−1,1). The single retained DC power agrees while the first adjacent product changes sign. `blind_edge` proves the obstruction for the actual single-frame Hann whenever the graph includes an edge incident to its zero endpoint. Flipping that endpoint in a two-impulse waveform preserves all powers and changes the edge product. No uniform positive lower gain holds on the unrestricted pilot waveform space. Whether the sampled measurements separate or stably control each pilot's restricted synthesis family is open. [Pilot geometry](03-finite-window-identifiability-brief.md)
- `powers_scale` and `no_positive_bound_on_ray` show the scaling obstruction on a class containing zero and arbitrarily small positive multiples of a nonzero waveform. The scalar power x² on |x|≥a>0 satisfies |x²−y²|≥2a d±(x,y) for every pair, including those with differing amplitude (`scalar_lower`).
- With each power in [0,U] and ε>0, `log_cell_bounds` and `log_vector_bounds` bound each log discrepancy and its Euclidean norm between the corresponding raw discrepancy divided by U+ε and by ε. A uniform raw gain κ therefore gives log gain κ/(U+ε). Given noise η and fitted residual ρ in the measured norm, `residual_error` bounds the waveform error by (η+ρ) divided by the gain in that norm. The continuous Gaussian-window theorem of Grohs and Rathmair uses a different measurement geometry and a data norm with derivatives. [Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/1706.04374v1)
