# Slice 3 — what the finite Hann and multiwindow measurements identify

**Question.** Which waveform differences are visible to the retained Fourier cells? The project has a concrete single-frame pilot and a proposal for several views of one recording. Give each observation map its own mathematical statement. [Sequence and Jaganathan source](README.md) · [Audio analysis](../audio-analysis-brief.md)

**Two reference operators.** Read the [pilot synthesizers](../../../receipts/latent-recovery/probe/Synthesizer.cs) and [window definitions](../../../receipts/latent-recovery/probe/Sweeps.cs). The resonance pilot has one 512-sample frame and 64 log-spaced frequencies, the harmonic pilot one 1024-sample frame and 128 linearly spaced frequencies. Both use a unit-energy periodic Hann window. Their recorded features are log(P + ε) with known positive ε. The cell-correlation sweep uses selected pairs of cells. For a proposed multiwindow theorem, give the finite list or indexed family of windows, starts, retained frequencies, boundary extension and signal class. A deterministic statement parameterized by those choices is sufficient.

1. **Exact equality of views.** For each retained cell j write Aⱼx = Σₜ aⱼₜxₜ and Pⱼ(x) = |Aⱼx|² for real x. Prove that the known additive logarithmic offset preserves equality of powers. Write Pⱼ(x) = xᵀQⱼx with Qⱼ = Re(aⱼ)Re(aⱼ)ᵀ + Im(aⱼ)Im(aⱼ)ᵀ. For a specified candidate class C, give the necessary and sufficient criterion: the kernel of H ↦ (⟨Qⱼ,H⟩)ⱼ contains no nonzero xxᵀ−yyᵀ with x,y ∈ C. Equality of the two outer products means equality up to real sign, including at zero.
2. **A cut in the waveform.** Split x = u+v into disjoint nonzero pieces and compare with u−v. Prove Pⱼ(u+v)−Pⱼ(u−v) = 4 Re((Aⱼu) conj(Aⱼv)). A relative sign is visible for this pair exactly when one retained cross-term is nonzero. If every effective window misses one piece, all cells agree. Give a window spanning both pieces whose retained frequency still has zero real cross-term, and a cell that distinguishes the same pair. For example, two unit impulses one sample apart in a rectangular frame have a vanishing cross-term at a quarter cycle/sample and a nonzero one at DC.
3. **Application and scope.** For the single-frame pilot exhibit the blind sample at the zero Hann endpoint and two distinct waveforms modulo sign with equal features. Check the indexing against the source. State explicitly whether a witness belongs to the synthesizer family. For a multiwindow family give an exact test for visibility of a pair of separated impulses, including the product of the two window values and the cosine at their lag. Apply it to at least one specified finite geometry and identify the extra premise needed to infer recovery of *all* waveforms in C.

4. **Positive control beyond two samples.** Pose a finite recovery theorem for a periodic Hann of length W ≥ 3, unit hop, a zero-extended finite waveform, every frame translation meeting the recording, and all bins of an F-point DFT with F ≥ 2W−1. Invert the powers in frequency to get each frame's aperiodic autocorrelation. At lag d the rows over frame starts are a finite convolution (up to reversal) of the sample products xₜxₜ₊d with the taper products gₛgₛ₊d. Prove injectivity of this convolution whenever the taper-product sequence is nonzero, for instance from the first nonzero coefficient of a finite convolution. For the periodic Hann this covers d from 0 through W−2. Deduce one sign per component of the nonzero support graph with edges at those positive lags, and check the converse using the effective window support. This is a concrete route to a longer-window theorem even if the pilot's selected frequencies cannot supply the lag rows.

**Done when** the general criterion, cut calculation, pilot witness and full-grid Hann claim are proved or corrected, and the report names the geometry and signal class for each conclusion. Compare the setup with Jaganathan et al.'s nonvanishing-window hypothesis, boundary frames, frequency sampling and almost-everywhere quantifier. Generic uniqueness for complex signals would also require a separate argument to specialize the exceptional set to a real signal class.
